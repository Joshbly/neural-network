    What to watch for

    1. Watch the pros: the 20 best of 53 drivers from offline training (evolution strategies: solo pace first, then racing ever-stronger rival fields on 16 randomised tracks), picked by 80 ten-lap, 20-car races on tracks none of them trained on. They've never seen this track either, and they race it anyway.
    2. Stock-car aero: sit in someone's draft to build a run; the speed carries for about a second after you pull out, enough to slingshot alongside. Glue yourself to their bumper and you push them faster too. Get your nose on their rear quarter and you side-draft them, slowing them down.
    3. A vs B: blue cars run the small 2-layer brain, violet cars the bigger 3-layer one. Watch the charts and the heat-win tally. In testing, B starts slower (evolution has 3× more weights to tune) and the small brain stays slightly ahead even after 150 generations.
    4. 10-lap races: long enough that grid position barely matters and there's time to work through traffic. Every pass earns a car what it costs the car it passed, so attackers learn to pass and leaders learn to defend.
    5. Mirrors & defending: cars can see behind them (the rear pink rays), feel how fast the car behind is closing, and know which side it's on. The top pro uses it: leading from pole on 12 unseen tracks it was passed 3 times with its mirrors and 34 times with them blacked out. It mostly defends by never letting the attacker get a run, with only a slight lean toward covering the attacker's side, not hard blocking.
    6. Rubbin's racing, until it isn't: bump-drafting is free and door-to-door rubbing only dents sheet metal (a touch of drag, no downforce lost), but damage climbs steeply with impact speed. A hard rear-end wrecks the hitter's splitter (front downforce gone, so it pushes wide in every fast corner); a spun car's spoiler goes and it gets loose; grinding a wall shreds the bodywork; a big hit bends the suspension or ends the race. Watch the "aero lost" readout.
    7. Early generations: a demolition derby. Brains pinball off walls and each other, and the damage slows them down.
    8. Clean driving emerges: walls hurt, so the wall-hit chart falls. The orange beads are the road ahead the focused car is reading, which is how it knows to brake before a hairpin it can't see yet.
    9. Blue cones are slipstreams. Watch cars tuck in behind on the straights, then pull out to pass. Following too closely in corners costs grip ("dirty air").
    10. Pink rays show a car spotting a rival. ★ marks a returning champion.
    11. Then race them yourself: you start mid-pack against the top 9.
